# Cooling Phase 5 — Hydroxide SN2 evidence review

2026-10-26. Scope: cooled **SN2 only**, under the existing actual-hydroxide reaction model. This is Phase 5 of the cooling plan, not a restart of the earlier website-development Phase 5.

## Outcome

**No cooled SN2 profile was admitted.** The evidence review and conservative safeguard increment are complete; cooled SN2 3D remains deferred at the evidence/applicability gate. This does not mean SN2 is impossible under cooling, or that the existing SN2 products are wrong.

There is genuine lower-temperature aqueous methyl-halide evidence. It does not establish a general cold polar-aprotic hydroxide model, secondary/benzylic outcomes or compatibility of arbitrary additional groups. The current condition selectors cannot silently be interpreted as the buffer/base formulation of a different experiment. No production reaction, reagent, substrate or stereo rule was changed to make a source fit.

Conditional 2D net products, mapped SN2 mechanism states and theoretical arithmetic remain available where the existing structure checks permit them. The previous non-cooled SN2 workflow remains working. No SN1, E1 or E2 profile was implemented or modified.

## 1. Primary-source evidence

### E13: Baesman and Miller (2005)

[Publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10874-005-1904-0), [official USGS record](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70028000), [author-uploaded text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/226511868_Laboratory_Determination_of_the_Carbon_Kinetic_Isotope_Effects_KIEs_for_Reactions_of_Methyl_Halides_with_Various_Nucleophiles_in_Solution). J. Atmospheric Chemistry **52**, 203–219.

Experimental methods, pp. **205–207**, and discussion **4.2** were available as indexed author-text extracts, not a complete visually inspected PDF. Methyl bromide/iodide hydrolysis was studied at **287 K** (13.85 °C) and 306 K. Base-hydrolysis solutions used phosphate buffers, with prose specifying pH 7.3/8.8; kinetics followed methyl-halide concentration and isotope changes over weeks/months. Methyl-chloride hydrolysis was not measured. Possible phosphate substitution is discussed. This is not an isolated-methanol preparation or a substituted-alkyl-halide stereo study. The indexed Table I renders one pH as 8.3, unlike the prose's 8.8; that discrepancy was not resolved from the original page and was not encoded. The publisher PDF request returned a subscription-preview HTML page, not a PDF.

**Admission finding:** useful aqueous methyl-halide/base-reaction evidence; insufficiently resolved formulation/product applicability for an executable generic cooled hydroxide profile. Do not transfer it to all solvents or all SN2 sites.

### Additional primary control: Adachi, Eguchi and Haoka (1974)

[Open paper](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jcej1968/7/5/7_5_364/_article), [four-page PDF](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jcej1968/7/5/7_5_364/_pdf/-char/en), DOI **10.1252/jcej.7.364**, pp. **364–367**. All four pages were visually inspected.

The work measures aqueous methyl iodide/NaOH chemistry at **25–65 °C**, not a cooled reaction. Page 365 reports methanol GC measurements and correspondence with iodide; page 366 distinguishes water hydrolysis from the hydroxide-consuming reaction. Table 1 and Figures 1–5 do not provide a below-ambient product profile. It supports product/stoichiometry reasoning, not extrapolated cold permission.

### Additional primary lead: Adachi, Eguchi and Haoka (1977)

[Open paper](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jnst1964/14/5/14_5_370/_article/-char/en), [six-page PDF](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jnst1964/14/5/14_5_370/_pdf/-char/en), DOI **10.3327/jnst.14.370**, pp. **370–375**. Full text and the relevant experimental pages/figures were inspected.

The semi-flow absorption study spans **288–311 K**. Pages 371–372 describe aqueous NaOH, UV exclusion, methanol GC and iodide analysis. Figure 2's illustrated reaction trace is at **306.7 K**, not cold. Figure 3 includes **288.2 K** physical-partition measurements. Page 374 separates water and hydroxide reactions and reuses the earlier kinetic constants; page 375 reports unresolved complications in separate long-duration equilibrium attempts. No isolated cold product/work-up or stereochemical substrate scope was established here.

**Admission finding:** meaningful product and cooler absorption evidence, but a cold dissolution point is not itself a separately validated hydroxide-only reaction endpoint. Do not label the 306.7 K trace as cooled, or turn the entire study's temperature span into a validated SN2 operating window.

### Other leads, not approval evidence

- [Moelwyn-Hughes (1949)](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspa.1949.0044), *The kinetics of certain reactions between methyl halides and anions in water*: a primary bibliographic/abstract lead. Complete experimental text was not retrieved; no exact cold result is attributed to it.
- [Panov et al. (1996), EPA bibliographic record](https://hero.epa.gov/reference/9555142/): mixed-solvent methyl-iodide kinetics beginning at 283 K are a lead only; neither the complete experimental article nor a matching endpoint/formulation was reviewed. No mixed-solvent profile was added.
- [Activated-carbon-mediated base hydrolysis (2017)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0926337317303065): a different surface/additive model with competing products, not the current no-added-activation pathway.

No full-paper access is claimed for E13 or the historical leads. No secondary source's methyl-carbocation explanation was imported: ordinary methyl substrates remain excluded from SN1/E1.

## 2. Chemistry versus software scope

| Question | Finding / retained boundary |
| --- | --- |
| Can methyl halides react in cooler water? | Yes, the evidence provides a real research lead; cooling is not a chemical impossibility flag. |
| Does protic solvation forbid SN2? | No. The existing relative-solvation caution is retained; it is not proof of no reaction or an elimination winner. |
| Does methyl halide support E2? | No β-carbon/β-H exists. Tests ensure methyl cases do not acquire E2, E1 or SN1. |
| Does methyl evidence qualify primary/secondary/benzylic sites? | Not automatically. Those have different hindrance, competing chemistry and stereo requirements. |
| Can a generic polar-aprotic selector stand for phosphate-buffered water? | No. They are distinct media; the experiment cannot be silently substituted. |
| Does an inversion-correct graph prove cooled selectivity? | No. Physical inversion validates the net SN2 model, not experimental competition or efficiency. |
| Can 3D establish cold conversion, time or yield? | No. It is only a theoretical conformer of an eligible molecular product. |

This decision does **not** require a high preparative yield, complete conversion or an isolated product for every future theoretical model. It requires enough directly applicable reaction-stage/product evidence to define an honest bounded profile. That applicability was not established here. Nor is a numeric-temperature or concentration UI inherently required: an explicitly documented named assumption can suffice if independently reviewed and compatible with existing safeguards.

To revisit admission, obtain the complete E13 experimental pages/analysis and a directly applicable cooled aqueous-OH product/formulation study, then assess the smallest structure-based methyl profile first. Additional primary/secondary or benzylic scope needs separate evidence. Do not add buffers, phase-transfer catalysts, surfaces or new nucleophiles through a temperature label.

A future positive SN2 profile also needs a correctly versioned backend/frontend confidence contract. The current cooled legacy SN2 serializer intentionally reports conditional 2D and `canGenerate3D: false`; simply registering a name or bypassing one run guard would be inconsistent. No such bypass or speculative architecture change was made.

## 4. Independent verification contracts

The new tests use independently specified products for methyl Cl/Br/I, long primary, benzylic, reacting chiral, distant CIP-relabeling, remote ether/alcohol and multiple-site structures. Both input orders are exercised. These are **existing conditional-net preservation fixtures**, not claims that all are validated cold experiments.

Checked:

- Actual OH oxygen forms C–O while the selected C–X bond breaks. Oxygen charge changes −1 to 0; leaving halogen changes 0 to −1. Every represented atom is conserved and total charge stays −1.
- 2D includes alcohol and halide byproduct. The independently exercised ambient 3D contains the main neutral alcohol, not the departing ion. Their atom links agree.
- The mechanism remains one concerted SN2 step with two electron moves, no carbocation intermediate, no invented proton transfer and no activation-energy numbers. Cold conditional mechanism states match ambient states; they do not claim experimentally observed cold intermediates.
- Specified reacting-center physical inversion and independently recomputed CIP are preserved. A distant R→S relabeling can occur without physical inversion. Unspecified centers remain unassigned.
- Tertiary, neopentyl, ring-bound, allylic, aryl/vinyl, fluoride, short O-closure and unvalidated ester cases remain rejected for their existing specific reasons.
- Other eligible sites stay selectable. Extra sites/groups do not become experimentally inert or selectively converted because cooling was chosen.
- Authenticated match/diagram APIs retain conditional 2D/mechanisms; cooled run rejects before conformer embedding, including stale ambient IDs and forged profile fields.
- Generic aqueous/dry/alcohol/aprotic settings and added activation cannot approve the unreviewed profile. Missing medium and extra-activation uncertainty remain separately represented.
- Actual OH, NaOH and KOH calculator fixtures retain **full salt molar masses** and correct 1:1 theoretical amounts. Arithmetic never grants cooled 3D permission or predicts an experimental yield.
